Our current goal is: given a fractional solution to the flat norm LP, determine the integral solution to the ILP in polynomial time. On [[2026-07-21]] I gave a so called "tangent-descent" method which walked through the parameter space in order to find an integral solution. While it happened to work in this particular instance, I don't think it will work in general. Mainly, I do not think the approach is sound and it assumes that every fractional solution has a corresponding integral solution with the same objective, which I do not believe to be true.

Notice that two basic solutions $u_1$ and $u_2$ are **simultaneously optimal** for parameters $\theta=(\mathbf{v},\lambda\mathbf{v})$ if $\langle \theta, g(u_1)-g(u_2)\rangle=0$ where $g(u)=(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$. Thus, given fractional, optimal $u_1$ we seek $u_2$ such that $\langle\theta, g(u_1)-g(u_2)\rangle=0$. Thus, instead of changing $\theta$, we should find $u_2\in\Z^{N}$ such that $g(u_1)-g(u_2)$ is orthogonal to $\theta$. This leads to the following ILP:
$$
\begin{align*}
	&\textup{minimize} & \|g(u_2)\|_1 \\
	&\textup{subject to} & \langle \theta,g(u_1)-g(u_2)\rangle = 0 \\
	&& \mathbf{x}-[\partial]\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{c} \\
	&& u_2 = (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\ge 0
\end{align*}
$$
which is just as hard as solving it as an ILP from the start.

Borrowing from [[2026-07-21]], let $u'=u + \Delta u$ where $\Delta u = (\epsilon^+,\epsilon^-,\delta^+,\delta^-)$. Then it follows that
$$
	\langle\theta,g(u')-g(u)\rangle = \langle\theta,g(u)+g(\Delta)-g(u))\rangle = \langle \theta,g(\Delta u)\rangle
$$
as $g$ is linear. Then we can rewrite our ILP as follows:
$$
\begin{align*}
	&\textup{minimize} & \|\Delta u\|_1 \\
	&\textup{subject to} & \langle\theta,g(\Delta u)\rangle = 0 \\
	&& (\mathbf{x}+\epsilon)-B(\mathbf{y}+\delta)=\mathbf{c} \\
	&& u + \Delta u \in \Z^N \\
	&& \Delta u = (\epsilon^+,\epsilon^-,\delta^+,\delta^-)\ge0
\end{align*}
$$
But this still has our unfriendly integer constraint $u+\Delta u\in\Z^N$. 

Consider the homology constraints:
$$
\begin{align*}
	&&(\mathbf{x}^+ + \epsilon^+-\mathbf{x}^--\epsilon^-) - B(\mathbf{y}^++\delta^+-\mathbf{y}^--\delta^-)-\mathbf{c} &= 0 \\
	-&&(\mathbf{x}^+-\mathbf{x}^-) - B(\mathbf{y}^+-\mathbf{y}^-) - \mathbf{c} &= 0 \\\hline
	&&(\epsilon^+-\epsilon^-) - B(\delta^+-\delta^-) &= 0
\end{align*}
$$
implying that the set of $\Delta u$ which preserve the homology must satisfy the equation
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
	I & -I & -B & B
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon^+\\\epsilon^-\\\delta^+\\\delta^-\end{bmatrix} = 0,
$$
i.e., belong to the kernel of $\begin{bmatrix} I & -I & -B & -B\end{bmatrix}$. Define $r=\epsilon^-$, $q=\delta^-$, $z=\delta^+-\delta^-$, then it follows that
$$
\textup{ker}A = \left\{
	\begin{bmatrix}
		r + Bz \\
		r \\
		q + z \\
		q
	\end{bmatrix}:r\in\R^m,~q,z\in\R^n
\right\}
$$
which implies that $\textup{nullity}A = m+2n$. Indeed, I claim the basis for $A$ is given by
$$
	\Gamma = \begin{bmatrix}
		I_m & 0 & B \\
		I_m & 0 & 0 \\
		0 & I_n & I_n \\
		0 & I_n & 0
	\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Notice that
$$
\begin{align*}
	A\Gamma &= 
	\begin{bmatrix}
		I_m & -I_m & -B & B
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		I_m & 0 & B \\
		I_m & 0 & 0 \\
		0 & I_n & I_n \\
		0 & I_n & 0
	\end{bmatrix} \\
	&= \begin{bmatrix}
		I_m - I_m \\
		-B + B \\
		B - B
	\end{bmatrix}\\
	&= 0.
\end{align*}
$$
Thus, any homology preserving perturbation is of the form
$$
\Delta u = \begin{bmatrix}
	I_m & 0 & B \\
	I_m & 0 & 0 \\
	0 & I_n & I_n \\
	0 & I_n & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} r \\ q \\ z\end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix}
	r + Bz \\
	r \\
	q + z \\
	q
\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Looking at the integrality constraint, we get that
$$
\begin{align*}
	u' \in \Z^{2N} &\Longleftrightarrow u + \Delta u\in\Z^{2N} \\
	&\Longleftrightarrow \Delta u\equiv -u\bmod \Z \\
	&\Longleftrightarrow\begin{bmatrix}
	r + Bz \\
	r \\
	q + z \\
	q
\end{bmatrix}\equiv
-\begin{bmatrix}x^+\\ x^-\\ y^+\\ y^-\end{bmatrix}
\bmod\Z.
\end{align*}
$$
Combining equations, we get that
$$
	\begin{bmatrix}
		Bz \\
		z
	\end{bmatrix}
	\equiv
	\begin{bmatrix}
	x^- - x^+ \\
	y^- - y^+
	\end{bmatrix}
	\bmod\Z.
$$
The second equation is easily satisfiable by saying that
$$
	z = y^- - y^+ + k_1
$$
for some $k_1\in\Z^n$. Solving for $r$ gives us that
$$
\begin{align*}
	r &= -Bz - x^+ + k_2 \\
	&= -B(y^--y^++k_1) - x^+ + k_2
\end{align*}
$$
for some $k_2\in\Z^m$. Solving for $q$ gives us that
$$
\begin{align*}
	q &= -z - y^{+} + k_3 \\
	&= (y^+-y^--k_1)-y^+ + k_3 \\
	&= -y^--k_1+k_3
\end{align*}
$$
for $k_3\in\Z^n$. Thus, our search space is restricted to
$$
	\Omega = \left\{\begin{bmatrix}
		-B(y^--y^++k_1)-x^++k_2 \\
		-y^--k_1+k_3 \\
		y^--y^++k_1
	\end{bmatrix}:k_2\in\Z^m,~k_1,k_3\in\Z^n\right\}
$$
which can be reexpressed by $\Omega=\alpha_0+K\Z^{m+2n}$ where
$$
\alpha_0 = \begin{bmatrix}
	-B(y^--y^++k_1)-x^++k_2 \\
	-y^--k_1+k_3 \\
	y^--y^++k_1
\end{bmatrix}
~\text{ and }~
K = \begin{bmatrix}
	-B & I_m & 0 \\
	-I_n & 0 & I_n \\
	I_n & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Therefore, our new repair ILP is given by
$$
\begin{align*}
	&\textup{minimize} & \|d+Mk\|_1 \\
	&\textup{subject to} & \langle\theta,g(d+Mk)\rangle=0 \\
	&& d+Mk\ge 0\\
	&& k\in\Z^{m+2n}
\end{align*}
$$
where $d=\Gamma\alpha_0$ and $M=\Gamma K$. Unfortunately, this problem appears to still be NP-hard.

This also assumes that each fractional solution has an equivalent integral solution. We forgo this assumption by a slight tweak:
$$
\begin{align*}
	&\textup{minimize} & \langle\theta,g(d+Mk)\rangle \\
	&\textup{subject to} & d+Mk\ge 0\\
	&& k\in\Z^{m+2n}
\end{align*}
$$

Note that
$$
	\Delta u = d+Mk = \begin{bmatrix}
		k_2 - x^+ \\
		k_2 - x^+ - B(y^- - y^+ + k_1) \\
		k_3 - y^+ \\
		k_3 - y^- - k_1
	\end{bmatrix}
	=
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\epsilon^+ \\ \epsilon^- \\ \delta^+ \\ \delta^- 
	\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Thus, it follows that
$$
\begin{align*}
	g(d+Mk) &= \begin{bmatrix}
		e^+ + e^- \\
		\delta^+ + \delta^-
	\end{bmatrix}
	= 
	\begin{bmatrix}
		2k_2 - 2x^+ - B(y^--y^++k_1) \\
		2k_3 - y^+ - y^- - k_1
	\end{bmatrix},
\end{align*}
$$
i.e.,
$$
	\langle\theta,g(d+Mk)\rangle = \mathbf{w}^\top\left(2k_2-2x^+-B(y^--y^++k_1)\right) + \lambda\mathbf{v}^\top\left(2k_3 - y^+ - y^- - k_1\right).
$$
We can also use the constraint $d+Mk\ge0$ and the convexity of our objective to eliminate $k_2$ and $k_3$. That is, $k_2$ and $k_3$ are the smallest integers such that
$$
\begin{align*}
	k_2 &\ge \max\{x^+,x^++Bz\} \\
	k_3 &\ge \max\{y^+, y^- + k_1\}
\end{align*}
$$
where $z=y^--y^++k_1$ and max is taken elementwise. Thus, we may view $k_2$ and $k_3$ as functions of $k_1$ and all other terms are constants; so our objective can be expressed by
$$
	F(k_1) = \mathbf{w}^\top(2k_2(k_1)-2x^+-Bz) + \lambda\mathbf{v}^\top(2k_3(k_1)-y^+-y^--k_1).
$$
Which written as a sum is given by
$$
\begin{align*}
	F(k_1) &= \sum_{i=1}^mw_i(2\lceil x_i^++\max\{0,(Bz)_i\}\rceil - 2x_i^+ - (Bz)_i) \\
	&\phantom{=} + \lambda\sum_{j=1}^nv_j(2\lceil\max\{y_j^+,y_j^-+k_{1,j}\}\rceil-y_j^+-y_j^--k_{1,j}).
\end{align*}
$$
Leaving the following integer nonlinear program:
$$
	\min_{k_1\in\Z^n} F(k_1).
$$
