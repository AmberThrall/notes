Quick recap from [[2026-8-17]]:

**Lemma 1:** Given a fractional vertex $u=(x^+,x^-,y^+,y^-)$ to the flat norm LP, the closest integral vertex can be found by solving
$$
	\min_{d\in\Z^n}(\|w\odot(Bd-r)\|_1+\lambda\|v\odot d\|_1)
$$
where
$$
	r = \lceil x^+\rceil - \lceil x^-\rceil - B(\lceil y^+\rceil - \lceil y^-\rceil) - c.
$$

**Lemma 2:** If $\|r\|_\infty\le1/2$, then the closest integral vertex can be found by taking the ceiling of $y^+$ and $y^-$ and computing
$$
	x = c + B(\lceil y^+\rceil - \lceil y^-\rceil).
$$

Numerical experimentation suggests found that $\|r\|_\infty=0$ for each weight in our example. Which means we cannot apply Lemma 2 and have to focus on the restricted search space:
$$
	\cal{L} = \left\{d\in\Z^n: \|\mathbf{w}\odot(Bd-r)\|_1 + \lambda\|\mathbf{v}\odot d\|_1\le\|\mathbf{w}\odot r\|_1\right\}.
$$
Although it may be worthwhile using the alternative condition that 
$$
\lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 - \sum_{i=1}^nw_ih_i((Bd)_i)\le 0
$$
where $h_i(a) = |r_i| - |a-r_i|$.

Let $\lceil x^\pm\rceil = x^\pm + \epsilon^\pm$ and $\lceil y^\pm\rceil = y^\pm + \delta^\pm$. Notice that
$$
\begin{align*}
	r &= \lceil x^+\rceil - \lceil x^-\rceil - B(\lceil y^+\rceil - \lceil y^-\rceil) - c \\
	&= (x^++\epsilon^+)-(x^-+\epsilon^-) - B((y^++\delta^+)-(y^-+\delta^-)) - c \\
	&= (x^+-x^-)+(\epsilon^--\epsilon^-) - B(y^+-y^-) + B(\delta^+-\delta^-)-c \\
	&= \epsilon^+-\epsilon^--B(\delta^+-\delta^-).
\end{align*}
$$

Recall from [[2026-07-20]] that every vertex arises as follows: choose $T\subseteq[n]$ and $Z\subseteq[m]$ with $|Z|=|T|$ such that $\det B[Z,T]\ne0$, where $B[Z,T]$ is the $|Z|\times|T|$ submatrix of $B$ with rows $Z$ and columns $T$; set
$$
\begin{align*}
	y_T &= -B[Z,T]^{-1}c_Z & y_{[n]\setminus T}&=0 & x&=c+By.
\end{align*}
$$

Consider the Smith normal form for $B[Z,T]$,
$$
	S = SNF(B[Z,T]) = \textup{diag}(d_1, d_2,\dots, d_k)
$$
where $d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_k$. Then it follows that there are unimodular matrices $U$ and $V$ such that $UB[Z,T]V=S$. Thus, it follows that $y_T=-VS^{-1}Uc_Z$. Splitting $U$ into $k$ row-vectors $U_1,\dots,U_k$ gives us that
$$
	y_T = -V\begin{bmatrix}
		\frac{1}{d_1}U_1c_Z \\
		\frac{1}{d_2}U_2c_Z \\
		\vdots \\
		\frac{1}{d_k}U_kc_Z
	\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Let $\alpha_i=d_k/d_i$. Then we get that for $1\le j\le k$,
$$
	(y_T)_j = -\sum_{i=1}^kV_{ji}\frac{U_ic_Z}{d_i} = -\frac{1}{d_k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{ji}\alpha_iU_ic_Z = -\frac{Y_j}{d_k}.
$$
Define $\rho_j$ such that $\rho_j=Y_j\bmod d_k$. Then it follows that
$$
	\delta_j^+ = \begin{cases}
		\rho_j/d_k & Y_j < 0 \\
		0 & Y_j \ge 0
	\end{cases}
	~\text{ and }~
	\delta_j^- = \begin{cases}
		(d_k-\rho_j)/d_k & Y_j > 0,~\rho_j\ne0 \\
		0 & \text{otherwise}.
	\end{cases}
$$
Notice that $y$ is supported on $T$ so it follows that $By=B[[m],T]y_T$. Thus,
$$
	x_i = c_i + B[\{i\},T]y_T = c_i - B[\{i\},T]B[Z,T]^{-1}c_Z.
$$
Let $T=\{T_1,\dots,T_k\}$. Then we get that
$$
	x_j = c_j + \sum_{i=1}^kB_{j,T_i}(y_T)_j = c_j - \frac{1}{d_k}\sum_{i=1}^kB_{j,T_i}Y_j = \frac{X_j}{d_k}.
$$
Define $\tau_j$ such that $\tau_j=X_j\bmod d_k$; then
$$
	\epsilon_j^+ = \begin{cases}
		(d_k-\tau_j)/d_k & X_j > 0,~\tau_j\ne0 \\
		0 & \text{otherwise}
	\end{cases}
	~\text{ and }~
	\epsilon_j^- = \begin{cases}
		\tau_j/d_k & X_j < 0 \\
		0 & \text{otherwise}.
	\end{cases}
$$


**Proposition 1:** Assume that the optimal vertex of the flat norm LP has $d_k=2$, and let $T'\subseteq T$ be the subcomplex consisting of the $p$-simplices with fractional values. Then it follows that
$$
	\|r\|_\infty \le \frac{1}{2}\left(1+\left\|\partial_p T'\right\|_\infty\right).
$$
In particular, if $K$ is a non-orientable manifold, then $\|r\|_\infty\le 3/2$.

*Proof.* Note that $\delta_i=\delta^+-\delta^-=\pm1/2$ for all $i\in T'$. In such a case,
$$
	|(B\delta)_j| = \left|\sum_{i=1}^nB_{ji}\delta_i\right| = \frac{1}{2}\left|\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}\right|.
$$
Notice that $\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji} = (B\mathbf{1}_{T'})_j=(\partial T')_j$, i.e., the row sum corresponds to the $(p-1)$-simplex $\sigma_j$ where $\sigma=\partial T'$. So we get that
$$
	\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji} = \#\{\text{cofaces of $\sigma_j$ in $T'$ with sign $+1$}\} - \#\{\text{cofaces of $\sigma_j$ in $T'$ with sign $-1$}\}.
$$
Let $\cal{C}_{T'}(\sigma_j)=\{\tau_i\in T':\sigma_j\subset \tau_i\}$. If $K$ is a non-orientable manifold, then we get that
$$
	\left|\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}\right| = \left|\sum_{i\in \cal{C}_{T'}(\sigma_j)}B_{ji}\right| \le \sum_{i\in \cal{C}_{T'}(\sigma_j)}|B_{ji}| =\sum_{i\in \cal{C}_{T'}(\sigma_j)}1 = \#\cal{C}_{T'}(\sigma_j)\le2.
$$
Note that $Y_j\in 2\Z+1$ for $j\in T'$ and $Y_j\in 2\Z$ for $j\not\in T'$. Moreover
$$
\begin{align*}
	X_j &= 2c_j - \sum_{i=1}^kB_{j,T_i}Y_j \\
	 &= 2c_j - \sum_{i=1}^kB_{j,T_i}Y_j \\
	 &= 2c_j - 2\sum_{i=1}^kB_{j,T_i}z_j - \sum_{i\in T'}B_{j,T_i} \\
	 &= \underbrace{2c_j - 2\sum_{i=1}^kB_{j,T_i}z_j}_{\in2\Z} - 2(B\delta)_j.
\end{align*}
$$
So we get that $\tau_j=\left(-2(B\delta)_j\right)\bmod2$. Working through the six possible combinations of values of $\epsilon^\pm$ gives us a value of $r_j$ given in the tables below:

If $(B\delta)_j\ge0$:

|            | $X_j>0$                                          | $X_j<0$                                           | $X_j=0$                            |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| $\tau_j=0$ | $-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}$               | $-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}$                | $-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}$ |
| $\tau_j=1$ | $\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}\right)$ | $-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}\right)$ | $-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}$ |

If $(B\delta)_j<0$:

|            | $X_j>0$                                          | $X_j<0$                                           | $X_j=0$                            |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| $\tau_j=0$ | $\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}$                | $\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}$                 | $\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}$  |
| $\tau_j=1$ | $\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}\right)$ | $\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}\right)$ | $-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}$ |

Taking the absolute value gives us that
$$
	|r_j| \le \frac{1}{2}\left(1+\left|\sum_{i\in T'}B_{ji}\right|\right) \le \frac{1}{2}\left(1+\|\partial_pT'\|_\infty\right).
$$
Therefore, if $K$ is a non-orientable manifold, then $|r_j|\le3/2$. $\square$

**Corollary:** If every $(p-1)$-simplex $\sigma_j$ in $\partial T'$ is a face of exactly one $p$-simplex, then $\|r\|_\infty\le1$.

*Proof.* In such a case, $\#\cal{C}_{T'}(\sigma_j)=1$ for all $(p-1)$-simplices $\sigma_j$. $\square$

**Remark:** Notice that if $\partial_pT'=0$, then we get that $\|r\|_\infty=0$ which implies that $T'=\emptyset$. Therefore, that case would correspond to an integral solution from the flat norm LP.

**Remark:** The only way we get $\|r\|_\infty\le1/2$ is if $\|\partial_pT'\|_\infty=1$ and $X_j=0$ for all $1\le j\le m$. However, there may be another way to get $\cal{L}=\{0\}$.

**Update 9/3:**

Recall the alternative condition for feasibility of $d\in\Z^n$:
$$
f(d)=\lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 - \sum_{i=1}^nw_ih_i((Bd)_i)\le 0
$$
where $h_i(a) = |r_i| - |a-r_i|$.

**Proposition 2:** If 
$$
	\lambda\min_j v_j > (p+1)\max_iw_i
$$
then $d^*=0$. Consequently, we can find the integral solution by taking the ceiling of $y^\pm$.

*Proof.* First notice that
$$
	h_i(a) = \begin{cases}
		0 & a = 0 \\
		2|r_i|-|a| & a\ne0,~\textup{sgn}(a)=\textup{sgn}(r_i) \\
		-|a| & \text{otherwise}
	\end{cases}
	\Longrightarrow h_i = \begin{cases}
		2|r_i| - |a| & ar_i>0 \\
		-|a| & ar_i\le0
	\end{cases}
$$
as
$$
\begin{align*}
	|a-r_i| = \begin{cases}
		|r_i| & a = 0 \\
		|a|-|r_i| & a\ne0,~\textup{sgn}(a)=\textup{sgn}(r_i) \\
		|a|+|r_i| & \text{otherwise.}
	\end{cases}
\end{align*}
$$
Let $A(d)=\{i:(Bd)_ir_i>0\}$. Then it follows that
$$
\begin{align*}
	f(d) &= \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 - \sum_{i=1}^nw_ih_i((Bd)_i) \\
	&= \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 - \sum_{i\in A(d)}w_i(2|r_i|-|(Bd)_i|) + \sum_{i\not\in A(d)}w_i|(Bd)_i| \\
	&=  \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 + \sum_{i=1}^nw_i|(Bd)_i| - 2\sum_{i\in A(d)}w_i|r_i|.
\end{align*}
$$
Because $d\in\Z^n$, we get that $|(Bd)_i|\ge 1$. Thus,
$$
\begin{align*}
	f(d) &\ge \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 + \sum_{i=1}^nw_i - 2\sum_{i\in A(d)}w_i|r_i| \\
	&= \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 + \sum_{i\not\in A(d)}w_i + \sum_{i\in A(d)}w_i(1-2|r_i|) \\
	&\ge \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 + \sum_{i\in A(d)}w_i(1-2|r_i|)\\
	&\ge \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 + \sum_{i\in \text{supp}(Bd)}w_i(1-2|r_i|) \\
	&\ge \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 - \sum_{i\in \text{supp}(Bd)}w_i(2|r_i|-1)
\end{align*}
$$
where $\text{supp}(Bd)=\{i:(Bd)_i\ne0\}$. 

We also note that
$$
	\lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 \ge \lambda\left(\min_jv_j\right)\|d\|_1\ge \lambda\left(\min_jv_j\right)\|d\|_0
$$
where $\|d\|_0=\#\{i:d_i\ne0\}$. Because each column of $B$ has exactly $p+1$ non-zero entries, it follows that
$$
	\#\textup{supp}(Bd) \le (p+1)\|d\|_0.
$$
As a result,
$$
\begin{align*}
	f(d) &\ge \lambda\left(\min_jv_j\right)\|d\|_0 - \sum_{i\in\text{supp}(Bd)}w_i(2|r_i|-1) \\
	&\ge \lambda\left(\min_jv_j\right)\|d\|_0 - \sum_{i\in\text{supp}(Bd)}\max_i(w_i(2|r_i|-1)) \\
	&\ge \lambda\left(\min_jv_j\right)\|d\|_0 - \sum_{i\in\text{supp}(Bd)}\max_i(w_i) && (|r_i|\le 1)\\
	&\ge \lambda\left(\min_jv_j\right)\|d\|_0 - (p+1)\max_i(w_i)\|d\|_0 \\
	&= \|d\|_0\left(\lambda \min_jv_j - (p+1)\max_iw_i\right).
\end{align*}
$$
Therefore, whenever $d\ne0$ we get $\|d\|_0>0$ which implies that $f(d)>0$. As a consequence, $d=0$ is the only feasible solution. $\square$

**Corollary:** Let $F(j)=\{i:B_{ij}\ne0\}$. If
$$
	\lambda v_j > \sum_{i\in F(j)}w_i
$$
for all $1\le j\le n$, then $d^*=0$.

*Proof.* Using that $|r_i|\le 1$, we get that
$$
\begin{align*}
	f(d) &\ge \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 - \sum_{i\in \text{supp}(Bd)}w_i(2|r_i|-1) \\
	&\ge \lambda\|v\odot d\|_1 - \sum_{i\in\text{supp}(Bd)}w_i
\end{align*}
$$
Taking $d=\pm e_j$ gives us that $(Bd)_i\ne0$ only on $i\in F(j)$ which implies that 
$$
	f(\pm e_j) \ge \lambda v_j-\sum_{i\in F(j)}w_i.
$$
Using $e_1,\dots,e_n$ as a basis gives us that if $d\ne0$ then
$$
\begin{align*}
	f(d_1e_1+\cdots+d_ne_n) &\ge \lambda\sum_{j=1}^n|d_j|v_j-\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(Bd)}w_i \\
	&\ge \lambda\sum_{j\in\text{supp}(d)}|d_j|v_j - \sum_{j\in\text{supp}(d)}\sum_{i\in F(j)}w_i \\
	&\ge \sum_{j\in\text{supp}(d)}\left(\lambda v_j - \sum_{i\in F(j)}w_i\right). && (d_j\in\Z).
\end{align*}
$$
Therefore $d^*=0$ as $d=0$ is the only feasible solution. $\square$

**Remark:** From a topological point of view, the above corollary states that if the weight of every triangle (scaled by $\lambda$) is greater than the weights of its boundary edges, then $d^*=0$. Unfortuntely, as $\lambda<<1$ the above sufficient condition is likely rare to occur.3
