It was shown by Fiorini et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05947) that
$$
	\min\{c^\top x:Ax\le b,~x\in\Z^n\}
$$
is solvable in strongly polynomial time if $A$ is totally $\Delta$-modular and contains at most two nonzero entries in each row or in each column. This result assumes $\Delta$ is fixed, and the worst-case complexity blows up as $\Delta$ increases. They do not provide a worst-case complexity simply stating that for fixed $\Delta$ it is polynomial time which depends on $\Delta$, but the degree is enormous with no specific exponent provided. For that reason, it may be best to stick to Artmann et al.'s $\Delta=2$ case which has a more reasonable exponent (I believe it is degree 4. They make use of Seymour decomposition theorem).

The MSFN is of the form
$$
	\min\{c^\top(x^+,x^-,y^+,y^-):x^+-x^--By^+ + By^-=c,~x^+,x^-,y^+,y^-\in\Z_{\ge0}^*\}.
$$
However, the only crucial component for determining the vertices of the polytope is $y=y^+-y^-$ and $B$ if on a pseudometric satisfies the requirements for Fiorini et al. Can we perhaps encode the problem in a different way to get it in the correct form while preserving totally $\Delta$-modularity and at most two nonzero entries per row?

We can capture $|y_j|$ by introducing variable $z_j$with constraints
$$
\begin{align*}
	y_j - z_j &\le 0 \\
	z_j &\ge 0
\end{align*}
$$
and the objective terms to 
$$
	y^++y^-\longrightarrow 2z_j - y_j.
$$
Indeed, if $y_j<0$ then $z_j=0$ and the cost is $|y_j|$. If $y_j\ge0$, then $z_j=y_j$ and the cost is $|y_j|$.

For row $i$ of $B$, let $u(i)$ and $v(i)$ be the two non-zero positions with coefficients $\alpha_i,\beta_i\in\{\pm1\}$. Then it follows that 
$$
	B_iy = \alpha_iy_{u(i)}+\beta_iy_{v(i)}
$$
which implies that
$$
	x_i = b_i - B_iy = b_i - \alpha_iy_{u(i)}-\beta_iy_{v(i)}.
$$
A classic trick to encode $|x_i|$ is by adding $t_i\ge|b_i-B_iy|$:
$$
\begin{align*}
	t_i &\ge b_i - B_iy = b_i - \alpha_i y_{u(i)} - \beta_iy_{v(i)} \\
	t_i &\ge B_iy - b_i = \alpha_iy_{u(i)} + \beta_i y_{v(i)} - b_i
\end{align*}
$$
and swapping $|x_i|$ with $t_i$ in the objective.

At this point, our problem looks like
$$
	\begin{bmatrix}
		I & -I & 0 \\
		0 & -I & 0 \\
		B & 0 & -I \\
		-B & 0 & -I \\
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		y\\ z\\ t
	\end{bmatrix}
	\le \begin{bmatrix}
		0 \\ 0 \\ -b \\ b
	\end{bmatrix}.
$$
While this preserves totally $\Delta$-modular, we now have three non-zero entries per row.

Define $B=B^1 + B^2$ such that $B_i^1$ corresponds to the $u(i)$ entries and $B_i^2$ corresponds to the $v(i)$ entries (if they exist, otherwise the row is just zero). Then we can split $x_i$ up as follows:
$$
\begin{align*}
	p_i &= B_i^1y = \alpha_iy_{u(i)} \\
	q_i &= B_i^2y = \beta_iy_{v(i)} \\
	x_i &= p_i + q_i.
\end{align*}
$$

**Claim:** $t_i\ge b_i-B_iy$ and $t_i\ge B_iy-b_i$ is equivalent to:
$$
\begin{align*}
	p_i &\le b_i - \alpha_iy_{u(i)} \\
	q_i &\le -\beta y_{v(i)} \\
	p_i + q_i &\le 0 \\
	p_i' &\le \alpha_iy_{u(i)} - b_i \\
	q_i' &\le \beta_iy_{v(i)} \\
	p_i' + q_i'&\le 0.
\end{align*}
$$
*Proof.* Adding the first two constraints gives us $p_i+q_i\le b_i-B_iy$. Likewise, adding constraints 4 and 5 gives us $p_i'+q_i'\le B_iy-b_i$. Through minimization, we get that
$$
\begin{align*}
	p_i + q_i &= \min(0, b_i-B_iy) \\
	p_i' + q_i' &= \min(0, B_iy-b_i).
\end{align*}
$$
Thus, $p_i+q_i$ and $p_i'+q_i'$ act identical to $x_i^+$ and $x_i^-$. Thus, we get that
$$
	\min|x_i| = \min\{-(p_i + q_i) - (p_i'+q_i')\}.
$$
$\square$

Assuming everything is correct, the new ILP is given by
$$
\begin{align*}
	&\text{min} & \sum_{i=1}^mw_i[-(p_i+q_i)-(p_i'+q_i')] + \lambda\sum_{j=1}^nv_j(2z_j-y_j) \\
	&\text{s.t.} & y_j - z_j &\le 0 \\
	&& -z_j &\le 0 \\
	&& p_i + B_i^1y &\le b_i \\
	&& q_i + B^2_iy &\le 0 \\
	&& p_i + q_i &\le 0 \\
	&& p_i' - B^1_iy &\le  -b_i \\
	&& q_i'- B^2_iy &\le 0 \\
	&& p_i' + q_i' &\le 0.
\end{align*}
$$
Therefore, the constraints in matrix form looks like
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
	I & -I & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
	0 & -I & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
	B^1 & 0 & I & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
	B^2 & 0 & 0 & I & 0 & 0 \\
	0 & 0 & I & I & 0 & 0 \\
	-B^1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & I & 0 \\
	-B^2 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & I\\
	0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & I & I\\
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
	y \\ z \\ p \\ q \\ p' \\ q'
\end{bmatrix}
\le
\begin{bmatrix}
	0 \\ 0 \\ b_i \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ -b_i \\ 0 \\ 0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$
which satisfies the conditions by Fiorini et al. as long as the boundary matrix $B$ does. Moreover, this matrix is (strictly) totally $\Delta$-modular if and only if $B$ is (strictly) totally $\Delta$-modular. The objective vector is given by
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
	-\lambda v^\top & 2\lambda v^\top & -w^\top & -w^\top & -w^\top & -w^\top 
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

**Lemma:** Let $A$ be totally $\Delta$-modular with at most two non-zero entries per row and pick a row $r=\alpha e_u+\beta e_v$ of $A$ with $\alpha,\beta\in\{-1,0,1\}$. Build the matrix $A'$ by the following steps:
1. Remove row $r$
2. Add two new columns $p$ and $q$
3. Add three rows: $r_1=\alpha e_u+e_p$, $r_2=\beta e_v+e_q$, and $r_3=e_p+e_q$.

It follows that $A'$ is totally $\Delta$-modular.

*Proof.* Let $S$ be a square submatrix of $A'$. Split the rows of $S$ into $R$ containing rows of $A$ that are not $r$, and added rows $R'\subseteq\{r_1,r_2,r_3\}$. We consider four cases on the new columns in $S$.

Case 1 (no new columns are added): If $r_3$ is in $R'$, then it follows that there is a zero row implying that $\det S=0$. Otherwise, $S$ is a submatrix of $A$ with potentially $\alpha e_u$ or $\beta e_v$ added. Thus, $|\det S|\le\Delta$ by Lemma 2.2.

Case 2 (only column $p$ is added): Column $p$ only shows up in $r_1$ and $r_3$. Clearly, if $S$ uses neither rows, then there is a zero column so $\det S=0$. Assume only $r_1$ is used. Then we get the submatrix
$$
	\begin{bmatrix} 
		\alpha & \cdots & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0
	\end{bmatrix}
$$
which can be eliminated by expanding along column $p$ giving a submatrix of $A$. We get a simiilar case if only $r_3$ is used. If $r_1$ and $r_3$ are both in $S$, then we get the submatrix
$$
	\begin{bmatrix} 
		\alpha & \cdots & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0 \\
		0 & \cdots & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0
	\end{bmatrix}
$$
Apply the row operation $r_1\leftarrow r_1-r_3$, which does not alter $\det S$. Then by expanding along column $p$, we get a submatrix $S'$ which is simply a submatrix of $A$ with $\alpha$ inserted along a single row. Thus, by case 1, $|\det S|=|\det S'|\le\Delta$.

Case 3 (only column $q$ is added): Same case as case 2 but with $r_2$ instead of $r_1$.

Case 4 (both $p$ and $q$): Notice that if $S$ contains less than two rows from $\{r_1,r_2,r_3\}$, then there will be at least one zero column along $p$ or $q$ giving $\det S=0$. We break into cases on which rows are in $S$. 
- $\{r_1,r_2\}$: then we have the following submatrix in $S$
$$
	\begin{bmatrix} 
		\alpha & \cdots & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0 \\
		0 & \cdots & \beta & \cdots & 0 & 1
	\end{bmatrix}.
$$
By expanding along $p$ and $q$ we have removed the additions giving a submatrix of $A$.
- $\{r_1,r_3\}$: then we have the following submatrix in $S$
$$
\begin{bmatrix} 
	\alpha & \cdots & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0 \\
	0 & \cdots & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$Expanding along column $q$ then column $p$ to remove the additions gives a submatrix of $A$.
- $\{r_2,r_3\}$: similar argument as $\{r_1,r_3\}$.
- $\{r_1,r_2,r_3\}$: then we have the following submatrix in $S$
$$
	\begin{bmatrix} 
		\alpha & \cdots & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0 \\
		0 & \cdots & \beta & \cdots & 0 & 1 \\
		0 & \cdots & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 1
	\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Perform the row operations $r_3\leftarrow r_3-r_1$ and $r_3\leftarrow r_3-r_2$ to get the new submatrix
$$
	\begin{bmatrix} 
		\alpha & \cdots & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0 \\
		0 & \cdots & \beta & \cdots & 0 & 1 \\
		-\alpha & \cdots & -\beta & \cdots & 0 & 0
	\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Expanding along columns $p$ and $q$ leaves a submatrix of $A$ in which row $r$ was negated. Thus, $|\det S|\le \Delta$.

Therefore, all cases give $|\det S|\le\Delta$. $\square$

**Claim:** If $B$ is totally $\Delta$-modular, so is the constraint matrix $M$.

*Proof.* Starting from $B$, we can build $M$ by the following steps:
1. For each row $B_i$, create a copy of $-B_i$
2. For each row $B_i$ apply the above lemma which gives the $p$ and $q$ blocks. Repeat for each row $-B_i$ to get the $p'$ and $q'$ blocks.
3. Add $-I$ blocks for $z$.
4. Apply row/column permutations.

All of the above operations preserve totally $\Delta$-modular. $\square$

**Question:** Is $M$ strictly totally $\Delta$-modular?

**Theorem:** Let $\Delta\ge1$ be a fixed integer. Then there is a strongly polynomial time algorithm for the subset of MSFN instances in which $|K|$ is a pseudomanifold and $[\partial_n]$ is totally $\Delta$-modular is solvable in strongly polynomial time.
